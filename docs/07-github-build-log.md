# 07 // GitHub Build Log Workflow

The goal is to document decisions and proof—not publish every raw screen, prompt, or command output.

## Milestone rhythm

For each build session:

1. **Intent** — state what you are changing or learning.
2. **Source** — record the official guide or trusted reference.
3. **Before** — capture the known-good state.
4. **Understanding** — explain the concept before receiving an AI answer.
5. **Action** — record concise steps.
6. **Test** — show how success or failure was determined.
7. **Recovery** — record one safe failure and fix when appropriate.
8. **Lesson** — explain what changed in your understanding.
9. **Proof** — record quiz, application, and independent repeat evidence.
10. **Next** — name one controlled milestone.

## Public AI evidence rules

Publish:

- Sanitized prompts
- Your first explanation
- Quiz scores
- Corrected concepts
- Verified source links
- Safe lab results
- AI limitations or mistakes discovered

Do not publish:

- Full private chat histories
- Credentials or tokens
- Real addresses or infrastructure identifiers
- Client, patient, family, or studio data
- Unredacted logs or configurations
- AI-generated commands you did not review or test

## Public narrative

**Beginning:** I built a small Ubuntu mini data center to learn the systems behind modern apps and cloud services.

**Middle:** I used AI to question and test my understanding, then proved each concept through real labs, failures, recovery, and repetition.

**End:** I could operate, rebuild, explain, and teach the system without depending on AI to do the work for me.

**Signal Lab extension:** I moved from software into physical systems—calculating circuits, measuring real behavior, studying antennas, and making signals visible through the Vision Node.
