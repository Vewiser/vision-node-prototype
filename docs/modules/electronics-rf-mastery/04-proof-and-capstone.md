# 04 // Proof 004 and Capstone

## Proof 004 // Electronics + RF Systems Builder

Proof 004 shows that the operator can move from theory to a safe, measured, explainable physical system.

## Required evidence

### Electronics

- Original low-voltage schematic and calculations for voltage, current, resistance, power, and tolerances
- Simulation with assumptions, as-built circuit, labeled test points, and predicted-versus-measured table
- One controlled fault, evidence-based diagnosis, repair, and retest

### Radio and antennas

- Frequency/wavelength calculations and receive-only spectrum observations
- One antenna model, one constructed receive antenna, and calibrated analyzer results or documented alternative
- Explanation of pattern, polarization, impedance, SWR, feed-line loss, and installation effects

### Rules and safety

- Current Technician practice-exam record and plain-language license explanation
- Bench risk assessment, station grounding/power plan, and RF-exposure evaluation before transmission
- Evidence that any transmission stayed within license privileges and current rules

### Integration and communication

- Vision Signal Console or equivalent Linux integration
- System diagram, bill of materials, rebuild/recovery runbook, and sanitized build log
- Two-minute beginner explanation, ten-minute technical walkthrough, and independent rebuild

## Capstone // VISION SIGNAL LAB 01

Build a compact station around the Vision Node with:

1. Protected low-voltage power
2. A sensor or original control circuit
3. A receive-only SDR path
4. A modeled and measured antenna
5. A local Linux dashboard or notebook
6. A safe failure and recovery demonstration
7. A branded physical element with clear labels and service access

Transmission is optional and requires the correct license, station review, RF-exposure evaluation, minimum practical power, and full compliance with current rules.

## Evaluation rubric

| Area | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Theory | Cannot explain | Explains with prompts | Transfers independently |
| Calculation | Missing/incorrect | Correct with help | Correct, checked, unit-aware |
| Design | Copied | Modified/explained | Original and justified |
| Measurement | Unstructured | Valid basics | Predicts, compares, interprets |
| Troubleshooting | Guesses | Uses some evidence | Isolates, repairs, verifies |
| RF knowledge | Recognizes terms | Explains blocks | Connects theory to measurements |
| Safety/rules | Misses gates | Follows checklist | Explains and adapts controls |
| Documentation | Incomplete | Repeatable with gaps | Reproducible and safe |
| AI discipline | Depends on answers | Uses hints | Audits AI; works independently |
| Teaching | Reads notes | Explains build | Teaches and handles questions |

**Pass:** 16/20 or better, with no zero in safety/rules, measurement, or troubleshooting.

Repeat weak concepts after approximately **1, 3, 7, 14, and 30 days**. At 30 days, rebuild or diagnose a circuit and explain one antenna measurement without copied instructions.

## Case-study story

**Beginning:** I wanted the Vision Node to understand the physical world—not only software.

**Middle:** I learned the theory, calculated the design, built it safely, measured reality, and corrected my assumptions.

**End:** The Node became a working electronics and radio lab I can rebuild, explain, and keep expanding.
